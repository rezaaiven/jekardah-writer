# Examples

## linkedin-article

Input:

> In today's rapidly evolving technological landscape, organizations are increasingly recognizing the importance of observability. It is worth noting that many teams struggle to implement it effectively. In this article, I will share three lessons from my experience.

Output:

> Most teams that "add observability" end up with dashboards nobody opens. I built three of those before one actually got used in an incident. Here's what changed between the third and the fourth.

What moved: opener, hedging, address, rhythm. What stayed: the claim ceiling (personal experience, three lessons), the article's scope.

## linkedin-post

Input:

> I have published a new article discussing the challenges of database migrations and the strategies that can be employed to mitigate downtime. I would greatly appreciate it if you would give it a read and share your thoughts.

Output:

> Our longest migration took 9 minutes of downtime. Our shortest took zero.
>
> The difference wasn't the database. It was the order we did the boring steps in.
>
> I wrote up the exact sequence — link in the article below.

The CTA intent (read the article) is preserved; the begging is not.

## Review Mode

Input:

> We're super stoked to announce our game-changing new feature!!! It literally revolutionizes how you'll think about data forever.

Review:

> Performed enthusiasm and unsupported hype. "Super stoked", triple exclamation, "game-changing", "literally revolutionizes", and "forever" all exceed what any body could pay off. A conversational register still needs a defensible claim: state what the feature does and for whom, then let one plain sentence carry the excitement.
